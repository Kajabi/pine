# Datepicker

Datepicker captures a date, or with `range` a start and end date, as an ISO `YYYY-MM-DD` value that never shifts by a day with the viewer's time zone. People type it or pick it from a calendar that opens from the button in the field. A range is `YYYY-MM-DD/YYYY-MM-DD`, and with `time-picker` each date carries a time, as `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:mm`. With `month-day`, for a date that comes round every year such as a birthday, it's a month and day with no year, `--MM-DD`.

## Guidelines

### When to use

- To capture one calendar date, such as a due date, a start date or a date in a filter.
- To capture a range of dates, with `range`, or a date and a time together, with the `time-picker` prop.
- When `min` and `max` bound the dates people can choose.
- For a date far in the past that people know by heart, such as a date of birth, with `show-dropdowns`. Paging back through months is slow; `show-dropdowns` jumps to the year, or people can type the date.

### When not to use

- For a time of day on its own, with no date. Pine has no time-only control, and `pds-input` has no `time` type; a [Select](/docs/components-select--docs) of times can stand in.

### Accessibility

- Always set `label`. Use `hide-label` to hide it visually; it stays available to assistive technology.
- The calendar is a modal dialog with a date grid, following the [WAI-ARIA date picker dialog pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/dialog-modal/examples/datepicker-dialog/).
- The button that opens the calendar is named by `trigger-label`, followed by the selected date. Set `trigger-label`, `previous-month-label` and `next-month-label` when the page isn't in English.

## Keyboard interactions

In the field:

- `Alt`+`↓` opens the calendar, or moves focus into it when a click has already opened it.

In the calendar:

- `←` and `→` move one day. The arrows follow the reading direction on right-to-left pages.
- `↑` and `↓` move one week, or in `month-day` mode one row of seven days.
- `Home` and `End` move to the first or last day of the week, or in `month-day` mode of the row.
- `Page Up` and `Page Down` move one month. With `Shift`, they move one year; `month-day` mode has no year to jump by, so `Shift` stays put.
- `Enter` or `Space` picks the day and closes the calendar. In range mode, it starts or ends the range. With `time-picker`, the date waits for Apply.
- `Escape` closes the calendar without changing the date.
- `Tab` moves between the presets, the month buttons, the day and, in range mode, Cancel and Apply.

### Format

`format` sets how the field shows dates and reads typed ones, with the tokens `YYYY`, `MMMM` or `MMM` (the month's name, in the locale), `MM` or `M` (month), `DD` or `D` (day), and for times `HH` or `H` (24-hour), `hh` or `h` (12-hour), `mm` and `A` (AM/PM). Put literal text in brackets: `[Due] D/M/YYYY` shows "Due 13/10/2026", and reads a typed date that includes "Due". Typed dates may drop leading zeros, and a typed ISO date is always accepted too.

The **value stays ISO** (`YYYY-MM-DD`, or `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:mm`; `--MM-DD` with `month-day`) whatever the format, so what a form submits and what the change event carries never depends on how the field reads. A `YYYY-MM-DD` or `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:mm` value can be parsed directly as ISO 8601 on the backend, as a calendar date rather than a midnight timestamp. `--MM-DD`, with no year, needs the handling below instead.

### Range

Set `range` to pick a start and an end from two months side by side. The field looks and works like a single date's: type the range as `YYYY-MM-DD – YYYY-MM-DD` (a hyphen, `to` or `/` between the dates also works), or click the field to open the calendar beside it. While it's open, typing moves the calendar to the typed range.

In the calendar, the first pick starts the range and the second ends it; until then, the range follows the pointer or the keyboard, and the footer shows what Apply will set. Nothing changes until Apply; Cancel or `Escape` keeps the range as it was. Picking only a start applies a one-day range.

### Range with presets

`presets` lists ranges beside the calendar, followed by **Custom range**, which is marked when the range matches none of them and moves focus to the calendar. The change event carries the matching preset's `key`, so a filter can keep "Last 30 days" relative to today rather than store fixed dates. Set `custom-range-label` to translate it.

Pass built-in keys as a comma-separated list: `today`, `yesterday`, `last-7-days`, `last-30-days`, `last-90-days`, `this-month`, `last-month`, `this-quarter`, `last-quarter`, `this-year`, `last-year`, `year-to-date` and `last-12-months`. "Last 7 days" is the 7 days ending today; "This month", "This quarter" and "This year" are the whole period, and "Year to date" ends today. To translate the labels or add ranges of your own, set the property to an array of `{ key, label?, startOffset?, endOffset?, start?, end? }`. A preset that falls outside `min` and `max` is disabled.

A range of your own can follow today as a built-in does, or sit on fixed days. `startOffset` and `endOffset` count whole days from today — `-6` is six days ago, `0` is today — and are read again every time the list is shown, so the range stays right however long the page stays open; set one and the other is today. `start` and `end` take `YYYY-MM-DD` days that never move, for a range that means one particular week.

Prefer the offsets for anything meant to stay relative. Fixed days never move, so a "last N days" range written as fixed dates is stale by the next day — and because a preset is matched by its exact range, the change event stops carrying its `key` too, leaving the panel on **Custom range**. A key of your own needs a `label`, as only built-in keys have one to fall back to. A preset with no label, or with neither a usable range nor a built-in key to take one from, is left out of the list, and says so once in the console.

The array form is a property, so set it from JavaScript or a framework, as in React: `presets={[{ key: 'last-7-days', label: '7 derniers jours' }, { key: 'last-14-days', label: 'Last 14 days', startOffset: -13 }, { key: 'launch', label: 'Launch week', start: '2026-03-02', end: '2026-03-08' }]}`.

`auto-apply` applies a range as soon as its end, or a preset, is picked, with no Apply or Cancel buttons. `Escape` leaves the range as it was, and so does a click outside after picking only a start. With `clearable`, the field's clear button is the way to empty it, since there's no footer.

### Range with times

Unless `format` includes a time, the field adds one on the same clock as the pickers, as `YYYY-MM-DD 2:00 PM – YYYY-MM-DD 10:00 PM` in `en-US`. Without `range`, `time-picker` adds one time, and the date waits for Apply.

### Month and day, without a year

`month-day` picks a date that comes round every year, such as a birthday or an anniversary. The value is a month and day as ISO 8601 writes one without a year, `--MM-DD`. The field shows it as the locale writes a month and day ("November 7", "7 novembre", "11月7日"), and reads that, the locale's numeric form ("11/7" in English, "7/11" in British English), `--MM-DD`, or a full date, whose year it drops.

The calendar shows each month with no year in its heading. A month without a year has no weekdays, so the days run in rows of seven from the 1st, with no weekday names; ↑ and ↓ move seven days, and Home and End go to the first and last day of the row. December is followed by January, and February always has its 29th. `range`, `time-picker`, `show-dropdowns`, `min` and `max` don't apply, and are ignored.

## Methods

- `open()` opens the calendar and moves focus to the selected day (a range's end), or today, kept within `min` and `max`. It does nothing while the datepicker is `disabled` or `readonly`.
- `close()` closes the calendar without moving focus. Focus inside the calendar is lost with it, so call `setFocus()` first to keep focus in the field.
- `setFocus()` moves focus to the text field.

## Inside modals, popovers and other overlays

The datepicker works inside `pds-modal`, `pds-popover`, `pds-filter` and `pds-drawer`. `Escape` closes only the calendar, never the overlay around it, and picking a day doesn't dismiss the overlay. The calendar sizes itself to the window, scrolling with its footer pinned when there isn't room, and hides when its field scrolls out of view. On narrow screens a range shows one month at a time.

The open calendar is portaled: into the `<dialog>` or open popover around the datepicker, or else to the end of the page. To style its `calendar` and `presets` parts, target the portal: `.pds-datepicker-calendar-portal::part(calendar)`.

## Values, time zones and servers

- Values are plain ISO strings with no time zone: `YYYY-MM-DD`, and with the time picker `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:mm`, a wall-clock time. If the time needs a zone, attach it on the server when parsing, such as the account's time zone.
- A month and day, from `month-day`, is `--MM-DD`, with no year to attach a zone to, and isn't a date. Don't pass it to a date parser: parsers that accept it fill in a year, usually the current one, which fails on `--02-29` outside a leap year. Read the month and day from the string instead, as `value.match(/--(\d{2})-(\d{2})/)`; if you need a date, put it in a leap year so February 29 is valid.
- A range submits as one field, `start/end`. Split it on `/` on the server and parse each half as ISO 8601: a `YYYY-MM-DD` half as a calendar date, not a midnight timestamp.

## Form Integration

- Set `name` and the date is submitted with its form as `YYYY-MM-DD`, `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:mm` with `time-picker`, or `--MM-DD` in month-day mode. In range mode both dates are joined with a slash: `YYYY-MM-DD/YYYY-MM-DD`, or `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:mm/YYYY-MM-DDTHH:mm` with `time-picker`.
- Resetting the form clears the date, rather than restoring the `value` it started with.
- To follow the user's changes, listen for `pdsDatepickerChange` (`onPdsDatepickerChange` in React) and read `event.detail.value`. The text field inside the datepicker keeps its own `pdsInput`, `pdsChange`, `pdsFocus`, `pdsBlur` and `pdsKeyDown` events to itself.
- With `required` and no date, typed text that isn't a date, or a date outside `min` and `max` (typed or set in code), the form won't submit and the field shows as invalid. The form reports `badInput` for text that isn't a date and `rangeUnderflow` or `rangeOverflow` for dates outside the range, with the messages set by `invalid-date-message`, `min-date-message` and `max-date-message`; `{date}` in the last two is replaced by the bound.
- For a complete form, what each control submits and how to show validation errors, see [Using Pine form components](/docs/guides-forms--docs).
